Stories from the bar

Two chapters from two different eras of my career. Pick one.

Some experiences stay with you long after the last drink is served. During my time with Broken Shaker, the bar earned international recognition as one of the World’s 50 Best Bars, taking us to London in July 2017, October 2018, and July 2019.

More than an awards ceremony, each trip was an opportunity to represent Miami, connect with the international hospitality community, and exchange ideas with some of the industry’s most creative people. I also had the opportunity to guest bartend at Dandelyan, Madison rooftop bar, and The Ned, bringing a little of the Broken Shaker experience to London. On my second visit in 2018, Dandelyan claimed the No. 1 spot on The World's 50 Best Bars list. I like to think I had a little something to do with the timing.

Those years reinforced something I've always believed: hospitality is more than what's in the glass. It's about culture, creativity, and the connections we create along the way.

My work eventually moved beyond the bar and onto the road. As a consultant, I developed cocktail menus, built beverage programs, and trained bartenders across the country.

Projects included Capital One airport lounges at DFW, IAD, DEN, LAS, and JFK, where I navigated the complexities of a large, multi-tiered operation while delivering Michelin-starred-level cocktail experiences through the right tools, training, and operational standards. In New York, I developed cocktails for STARR Restaurants, including Michelin-starred Le Coucou and Buddakan, and collaborated with the legendary Russian Tea Room to modernize its cocktail program while preserving the nostalgia and character that make it an institution.

Whether the room is an iconic restaurant or a busy airport lounge, the goal is the same: an experience that feels authentic, memorable, and distinctly its own.

Beyond the Bar

Discovering the people, places, and traditions behind every pour.

At the source

On the road, 2020 onward

Some of my most memorable experiences in hospitality have taken place far from the bar. Working alongside distributors and spirits brands opened the door to the places where great products begin. In Michoacán, Mexico, I visited La Luna’s artisanal mezcal distillery in September, 2022.

Patrón invited me to explore their distillery on two occasions, including a visit during Día de los Muertos, October, 2019.

My work with Broken Shaker took me to Puerto Rico to scout a location for a potential bar pop-up. Along the way, I visited the Don Q and Bacardi distilleries, gaining a closer appreciation for the island's rum-making heritage and the traditions behind two iconic spirits.

After 2020, life on the road in a motorhome offered the freedom to explore at a slower pace and document the journey. Stops through California and Oregon brought visits to wineries and a deeper appreciation for the landscapes behind American winemaking.

Argentina gradually became another home base and an ongoing journey of discovery. From the energy of Buenos Aires to the vineyards of Mendoza, the lakes and mountains of Bariloche, and Ushuaia at the end of the world, each destination offered a different perspective on the country's extraordinary diversity. Further north, Iguazú and Salta completed a journey that continues to shape my connection to the country.

Understanding a spirit means understanding its landscape, its producers, and the culture that gives it character.

Skills & studies

Where it started: Courtland Vocational School

My interest in design began at Courtland Vocational School in Virginia, where I completed a two-year graphic design program and graduated at the top of my class. The curriculum balanced creative exploration with technical discipline, teaching me how to translate ideas into finished work.

From Adobe Photoshop and early web coding to screen printing and traditional darkroom photography, I explored a range of creative disciplines. More importantly, I learned to think visually, pay attention to detail, and understand the process behind good design.

Those skills followed me throughout my hospitality career. Menus, promotional materials, event flyers, and brand presentations became natural extensions of my work behind the bar — another way to approach business, branding, and creative problem-solving.

Today, that foundation continues to evolve through digital design and web development, allowing me to take ideas from initial concept to visual identity and finished digital experience.

The Bar Book

A personal collection of cocktails, stories, and the bars that shaped my journey through hospitality.

Behind The BarBeyond The Ordinary

Every bar has a story, and behind every great cocktail is usually a little more than one person's idea. The Bars That Made Me is a look back at the places, people, and creative experiences that shaped my journey through hospitality, from Atlanta to Miami and New York.

Bringing together original recipes, contributions from former colleagues, personal stories, and a visual archive of menus and inspiration, the collection celebrates the creativity that happens when talented people come together behind a bar. Each recipe is credited to its creator, recognizing the individuals whose ideas, techniques, and collaborations helped define these chapters.

Part cocktail collection, part personal retrospective, the book revisits the drinks, the places, and the people who made the experience memorable. It's a tribute to the craft, the friendships, and the shared creativity that make hospitality such a remarkable industry.

Recipe & spec template

Every great cocktail starts with a clear, repeatable recipe. Use this template to document ingredients, exact measurements, preparation methods, glassware, garnish and service notes. Keep your team working from the same specifications and build a consistent foundation for menu development, training and day-to-day service.

Inventory & Ordering Guide

Keep your inventory organized and your purchasing consistent. Record quantities on hand by product category or storage location, establish par levels for essential ingredients and supplies, and compare current stock against your targets to identify what needs to be reordered. Track product movement, monitor usage patterns, spot discrepancies and organize purchasing by vendor. A reliable inventory routine helps reduce overstocking, prevent shortages and make smarter purchasing decisions.
